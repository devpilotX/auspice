# The labelling protocol

This directory holds the labelled decision dataset. It is the most valuable thing in the
repository and the only part that cannot be regenerated by running code.

Section 12 of the specification puts labelling on day 2, before any pipeline work, and gives
the reason: without labels there is nothing to validate against, and a pipeline built before
the labels exist will collect the wrong things. Labels before pipelines is the most commonly
inverted decision in projects like this.

## What counts as a labelled decision

One application to one body for one relief, that reached a terminal outcome or is still
pending. Every row needs:

- The jurisdiction, from the registry, by slug.
- The case number as the jurisdiction publishes it. Not one we invented.
- The use class and every relief sought, from the controlled vocabulary in
  `src/auspice/domain.py`.
- The filing date and the decision date. A decision with no date is not usable for the survival
  model, and the survival model is half the product.
- The outcome, from the controlled vocabulary.
- At least one citation: a URL, the document it points at, and a verbatim quote from that
  document that supports the outcome.

## Per member votes

Optional, and the highest value optional field in the format. When the minutes name who voted
which way, record it:

```yaml
    vote_for: 5
    vote_against: 3
    member_votes:
      - name: Supervisor Ellery
        position: for
        seat: Broad Run District
        term_start: 2024-01-01
        term_end: 2028-01-01
      - name: Supervisor Nakamura
        position: absent
```

`position` is one of `for`, `against`, `abstain`, `absent`, `recused`. Absent and recused are not
votes and do not count toward the tally.

Three features can populate from nothing else: `board_composition_score`, `swing_seat_count` and
`turnover_since_last_comparable`. Without per member records they return unknown for every row no
matter how many rows are labelled, because they are computed from the `vote` and `decision_maker`
tables and an aggregate tally never reaches those tables.

Two checks worth knowing about, because both refuse the row rather than warn:

An aggregate tally and a per member list are two transcriptions of one vote. If they disagree, one
of them is wrong, and loading both would put a contradiction into the training set.

A member whose recorded term does not cover the decision date cannot have voted on it. Without that
check the row would load, look complete, and quietly contribute nothing to the features, because the
feature queries filter members by term.

Section 8.9 forbids predicting how a named individual will vote. This records how they did vote, from
the public record, and every feature built on it is an aggregate. That is the difference between
reading minutes and profiling a person.

Name spellings vary between documents. Record the name as the source spells it. The loader matches on
the normalised name within the body and keeps every spelling it has seen, so two spellings of one
supervisor stay one person with one record rather than becoming two with half each.

## The citation rule

A row without a citation is rejected by the loader, not flagged. This is the same rule the
extraction layer applies to language model output, applied to human work, for the same reason:
an unsourced claim in a training set is indistinguishable from a guess, and it will be
laundered into a published probability.

`auspice labels verify` fetches every citation URL, stores it in the content addressed raw
corpus, extracts the text, and checks that the quote appears verbatim. A quote that does not
match marks the row unverified, and unverified rows are excluded from training.

Quotes are transcribed exactly, including any typography the source used. Do not tidy them.

## Approval with conditions

Counted as approval. That is a real modelling choice with a real cost, because conditions can
be onerous enough to destroy the economics, and section 2.2 of the specification names that as
a distinct failure mode. The conditions are kept in the row so a later model can separate them.

Withdrawals are not counted as denials, and they are not discarded either. A high withdrawal
rate is the `withdrawal_rate` feature, which measures hidden denials: staff killing projects
quietly before a vote. Throwing withdrawals away would erase that signal.

## Pending applications

Recorded with `outcome: pending` and no decision date. These are right censored, not missing.
A project filed fourteen months ago with no decision is the information that at least fourteen
months have elapsed. Dropping those rows biases every timeline estimate optimistically, which
is exactly the direction that destroys customer trust.

Pending rows are also the input to the prediction ledger: they are what gets scored before an
outcome exists.

## How many are needed

`auspice eval kill-test` refuses to report a verdict below 400 labelled terminal decisions and
60 held out decisions. It prints `INSUFFICIENT DATA` instead.

That threshold is not arbitrary. Below it the confidence interval on the Brier skill score is
wide enough to contain both "clear signal" and "no signal", so a verdict computed there would
be a coin flip wearing a number. Someone would quote it.

`auspice labels stats` prints the current count and the gap.

## Current state, stated plainly

The file `decisions.yaml` holds the decisions verified so far against primary and contemporary
secondary sources. It is well short of 400. Until it reaches 400 the kill test will refuse to
return a verdict, and no accuracy claim of any kind is published.

Two routes to close the gap, and they are complementary:

1. Hand labelling, which is what section 12 day 2 describes. Roughly 30 to 60 rows per day per
   person working from agendas and minutes.
2. The extraction pipeline, once a language model key is configured. Stage 4 produces candidate
   rows with verified quotes; a human confirms or rejects each one. This is faster and it is
   not a substitute, because the golden set that measures extraction accuracy has to be
   hand made.

Neither route is a shortcut, and there is no third route. Generating plausible looking rows
would produce a kill test that passes and a company that does not work.
